The French & Indian War had begun badly for Americans, but it ended well. Here, the Anglo-Americans have driven the French out of Fort Duquesne. On the site would be built Fort Pitt, and then the city of Pittsburgh, named for English parliamentary leader William Pitt.

While the Brown Bess remained the predominant firearm, the rustic American on the right, wearing animal skins, carries an American Long Rifle. It is also known as a Pennsylvania-Kentucky rifle. The rifle was invented in the 1730s by German immigrant gunsmiths, who had come to Pennsylvania for religious freedom. They modified their heavy central European Jaeger rifles to adapt to American needs. The rifle became especially popular in the large area of the Ohio River Valley.

Whereas the bore (the interior of the barrel) of a musket is smooth, a rifle bore has spiral grooves that make a bullet spin on its axis. The rifling greatly improves the bullet’s aerodynamic stability, making it a fine gun for hunting, even at distances of 200 yards or more.

Compared to a Brown Bess musket, the Pennsylvania-Kentucky rifle was more difficult to manufacture, slower to reload, longer (and hence more accurate at distance), lower caliber, and lighter weight. It was superb for the conditions of the American forest.

![American circa-1770 flintlock long rifle, a long-barreled muzzle-loading firearm with a slender wooden stock, metal barrel, flintlock mechanism, and period hardware, shown in a full-length museum display view.](../img/itemimgs/j_graeff_flintlock_rifle2.webp)

Whereas rifles had never been popular in England, the [American Long Rifle](https://www.nramuseum.org/the-museum/the-galleries/the-road-to-american-liberty/case-21-the-flintlock/american-circa-1770-flintlock-long-rifle.aspx) became the first iconic American firearm.
