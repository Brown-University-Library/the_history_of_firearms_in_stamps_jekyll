Other than the Civil War, no American war elicited so much controversy on the American home front as the War in Vietnam---especially from 1968 onward, when it became clear that President Johnson’s optimistic claims about progress had been deceitful.

This stamp is part of the 1960s series from the Postal Service’s 1998--99 “Celebrate the Century” megaseries.

Americans had air superiority throughout the war. Here, some soldiers have been helicoptered over the jungle and delivered to a battle zone.

The American service rifle of the war was the M16. It remains a service rifle today, modified into the M4. Like its predecessor the M14, the M16 is an assault rifle, which means it is capable of selective fire. By using a selector switch, the user can fire semiautomatically (one shot per trigger press), or automatically (multiple shots by holding down the trigger).

The M16 was invented by the ArmaLite company in 1957. ArmaLite had been founded by aerospace engineers to use the latest techniques to make firearms lighter. It was dubbed the AR-15, since it was ArmaLite’s 15th firearms model. In 1959, ArmaLite sold the patent to Colt Manufacturing Company.

A semiautomatic-only version was introduced to the American citizen market in 1965. Although the patents have expired, Colt’s retains the right to the trade name for the “AR-15.” With various names, semiautomatic rifles based on the AR platform have become the most popular firearm in American history, and now constitute about 20% of firearms sold in a typical year. They are low recoil, easy to handle, ergonomically excellent, and highly customizable. However, in the most common AR caliber (.223), the bullets are so small that some states do not allow them for hunting deer or larger game.

In the early years, "AR-15" and "M16" were used interchangeably, although popular usage eventually settled on M16 for the selective fire rifle, and [AR-15](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/wwii,-korea,-vietnam-and-beyond-1940-to-present/case-42-guns-of-vietnam-and-desert-storm/colt-ar-15-semi-automatic-rifle.aspx) for the semiautomatic-only. The two firearms appear almost identical on the outside, except for the selector switch on the M16. The M16 can fire at least four times as fast as the AR-15, due to differences in internal components.
