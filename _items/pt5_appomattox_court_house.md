The famous scene of General Robert E. Lee’s surrender to General Ulysses Grant, at the village of Appomattox Court House.

![U.S. Starr Arms Company Model 1858 double-action percussion revolver. The firearm features a dark, blued steel frame, a six-inch round barrel, a six-shot cylinder, a unique curved loading lever beneath the barrel, and a smooth, curved one-piece walnut grip.](../img/itemimgs/u_s_starr_arms_co_1858_double_action.webp)

Officers typically carried handguns, as did artillerymen. So did many cavalrymen. Revolvers from 37 different companies were used in the war. The picture is of [U.S. Starr Arms Co 1858 Double Action](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/a-nation-asunder-1861-to-1865/case-16-union-pistols-and-revolvers/us-starr-arms-co-1858-double-action.aspx). On the Union side, Starr revolvers were eclipsed in popularity by only revolvers from Colt and Remington. This revolver’s cylinder is designed to be speedily removable, so that the user can swap out an empty cylinder for a preloaded one. “Double action” means that the revolver can be fired as fast as the user can press the trigger—as opposed to “single action,” which requires the user to cock the hammer with his or her thumb before each press of the trigger.
