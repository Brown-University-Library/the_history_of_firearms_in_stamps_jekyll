To celebrate America 250, the Postal Service released the 15-stamp sheet Battlefields of the American Revolution on April 16, 2026, at Minute Man National Historic Park, in Concord, Massachusetts. Five battlefields are depicted, all of them involving battles that were also subjects of stamps for America’s 150th or 200th anniversaries: Lexington &amp; Concord (1775), Bunker Hill (1775), Trenton (1776), Saratoga (1777), and Yorktown (1781). For each battlefield, there is one stamp with a photograph of the modern memorial site, plus a watercolor diptych of a battle scene, by Greg Harlin.

On the collectible sheet (not pictured here) that surrounds the stamps is the opening of Thomas Paine’s “The American Crisis,” published on December 23, 1776: “These are the times that try men’s souls; the summer soldier and the sunshine patriot will, in this crisis, shrink from the service of his country; but he that stands it now, deserves the love and thanks of man and woman.” Shortly before the December 26 Battle of Trenton, Paine’s essay was read to George Washington’s soldiers. The Americans routed the British, thus saving the cause of independence from imminent final defeat.
