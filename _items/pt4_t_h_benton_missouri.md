Painter Thomas Hart Benton (1889--1975) was a distant descendant of Missouri’s first U.S. Senator, and shared the same name. This was the first stamp that U.S. stamp that Bureau of Engraving and Printing (BEP) produced with its new Andreotti photogravure press. For the previous several years, the BEP had contracted with private firms for photogravure printing.

The stamp depicts the fraught relationship between Indians and the settlers crossing the Mississippi River to move west. The settlers are traveling by ox-drawn covered wagon. They appear startled and worried by the apparently unexpected arrival of an Indian. He bears a peace pipe, but are his intentions truly peaceful? The settlers are unsure.

Flintlock firearms were ignited by flint striking steel and raining a shower of sparks on gunpowder in a firing pan. The fire in the pan then traveled to the main gunpowder charge via a small touch hole. In 1805, the Scottish Rev. Alexander Forsyth invented percussion ignition. The gun’s hammer struck a small cap of fulminate that sat on a nipple. The explosion then ignited the main gunpowder charge inside the firearm. The percussion cap made firearms more powerful, more reliable, and faster to reload. The photo shows a [flintlock musket](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/the-american-west-1850-to-1900/case-18-hunting-and-military-arms-on-the-western-frontier/us-model-1816-percussion-conversion-musket.aspx) that was retrofitted for percussion ignition.
