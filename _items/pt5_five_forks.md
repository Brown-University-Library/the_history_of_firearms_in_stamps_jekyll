One of the last battles of the Civil War, Five Forks was an attempt by General Robert E. Lee’s Army of Northern Virginia to break out of a Union siege. The breakout was repulsed by Union cavalry led by Gen. Philip Sheridan (who was later elected President of the NRA).

![U.S. Spencer Model 1863 lever-action repeating carbine. The firearm features a dark wooden buttstock and matching fore-end, a short blued steel barrel, a dark metal receiver, an external hammer, and a distinctive curved trigger guard lever.](../img/itemimgs/spencer_1860.webp)

Cavalrymen preferred carbines (short rifles), which were easier to handle on horseback, and they preferred lever actions, which were the easiest rifles to reload while mounted. Among the leading cavalry rifles was the [U.S. Spencer Lever Action Repeating Carbine](https://www.nramuseum.org/guns/the-galleries/a-nation-asunder-1861-to-1865/case-15-union-muskets-and-rifles/spencer-model-1860-repeating-rifle.aspx
). Reloads were very fast, thanks to a special patented tube that poured seven fresh rounds of ammunition into the tubular magazine in the rifle’s buttstock.
